The Journey

Four passes, in the order Andrew Ng reads a paper

Each pass is a checklist of what to read and what to answer. Every item opens that part of the paper inside the app, with its own explanation, figures and tables. Each stage has a suggested time limit, so you don't sink an afternoon into a paper you should have skipped.

  1. 1 · 10 min

    Skim

    Title, abstract, figures. Predict the results before you see them.

  2. 2 · 2 min

    Triage

    Worth more time? Go deeper, or file it and move on.

  3. 3 · 30 min

    Outline

    Intro, conclusion, section headings. Map how the parts fit.

  4. 4 · 60 min

    Read it through

    The whole paper, maths skipped for now, as Ng suggests.

  5. 5 · 90 min

    Master it

    Maths back in. Note what didn't make sense instead of stalling.

  6. 6 · 10 min

    Debrief

    Ng's four questions: goal, key idea, what you can use, what to read next.

  7. 7 · open

    Beyond

    Re-derive the key result on paper. Rebuild it. Read what it cites.

Results you predicted in Pass 1 stay hidden until you've made your guesses, so the paper can still surprise you.

Reading

Made for reading the paper, with quizzes as a check

Every pack rebuilds the paper in the order you'd learn it: the problem, the core idea, each component, why it works, how it was trained, what it achieved. The Attention pack has 11 lessons and 34 steps, each at two reading levels and each citing its page.

When it says “look at Table 1”, Table 1 is right there

The paper's own figures and tables sit inside the step that discusses them, credited, where the licence allows. Simplified redraws are labelled as redraws, so you never confuse them with the original.

The game layer

XP for the real work, never for tapping through

Points come from the steps of Ng's method: ticking off a part of the paper, answering a question, deriving a result without the hint. The weekly goal is 3 papers, close to the 2 or 3 a week Ng recommends.

Trust and privacy

Packs are written by Claude, so the app helps you check them

  • Check the pack

    A short checklist before you trust a pack: spot-check 3 claims, 2 cards and 1 chart against the paper, then verify the links. Anything wrong becomes a fix request you paste back into the chat.

  • Every claim cites a page

    Lessons, claims and cards point to the page and section they come from. Analogies that aren't from the paper are labelled as analogies.

  • Checked on import

    Every pack is validated when you import it. SVGs with scripts, embedded images or outside links are rejected, and nothing inside them ever runs.

  • Fixes you can review

    Paste Claude's corrections and the app shows exactly what will change, checks the pack again and keeps the old version.

  • On your device only

    Your library, progress and cards stay on the device. The apps have no internet permission and make no network calls.

  • No PDF to read

    The app is the reading experience: lessons, figures and passes. The paper's PDF never has to be opened, and it's kept out of packs you share.

Get it

Build it and add your first paper in a few minutes

Paper Study isn't in an app store. Build it from the project folder: the Mac app needs no Xcode project, and the Android app installs on Android 12 or later.

Mac

Builds Paper Study.app. Drag it to Applications. ⌘O imports a pack.

./paper-study-app/macos/build.sh

Android

Builds the APK. Install it on your phone and import a pack folder or a .pack.json.

cd paper-study-app/android && ./gradlew assembleRelease

Browser

Serve the folder locally, then open localhost:8765/paper-study-app/ in Chrome, Edge, Arc or Safari.

python3 -m http.server 8765 --bind 127.0.0.1

Make a pack for any paper

A pack is one paper turned into lessons, questions, cards and visuals. Claude writes it from a prompt, and the pack tool checks it. Full guide: paper-study-app/docs/GENERATING-PACKS.md.

  1. Start it from arXiv. pack.mjs new <link> --depth use drafts the manifest and section map, and writes the message to send.

  2. Let Claude build it in rounds. With the generator prompt in a Claude Project, reply “continue” after each round.

  3. Check it. pack.mjs validate --strict shows the same issues the app would.

  4. Fix and import. Paste the issues back, apply Claude's reply with pack.mjs apply, then import the folder.
